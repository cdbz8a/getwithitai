The curriculum

Eighteen modules in four blocks.

About 60 percent of the day is hands-on practice. Each module runs about 20 minutes, with breaks between blocks.

Block 1 · Foundations

Morning. Getting comfortable with it and learning how it works.

1

Welcome and task harvest

Everyone writes down the jobs that eat up their week, like emails, minutes, reports and forms. That list becomes your practice material for the day, so every exercise uses your own work.

2

The wake-up call

The whole room tries ELIZA, a chatbot from the 1960s, then asks a modern AI app the same questions. It shows you sixty years of progress in about ten minutes.

3

What AI is and how it gets things wrong

How it predicts words, and why it sounds sure of itself even when it's wrong. Then we play Stump the Machine, where the room comes up with an oddly specific request and watches what AI does with it.

4

Choosing your tools

An honest look at ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity and what each one is good for. You pick a main app and a backup that suit your kind of work.

5

Memory and context

Why it forgets things partway through a long chat, and when it's time to start a fresh conversation. This explains most of the strange results people run into.

Block 2 · Prompting

Late morning. Every other module depends on this skill.

6

The four-part prompt

The job, who it's for, the shape and the background. You practice it on your own task list until you do it without thinking.

7

Steering and follow-ups

How to tell it what to change until the draft is right. You'll also learn the interview method, where AI asks you questions before it writes anything.

8

Roles, examples and templates

Give it a role and show it an example of what good looks like. Then save templates for the jobs you do every week.

9

Making it sound like you

Teach it your voice using your own writing, and cut out the words that give AI away, so people still hear you when they read it.

Block 3 · Real work

Afternoon. The main work modules, done on your own material.

10

Email and hard messages

Replies, follow-ups, saying no politely, and the message you've been dreading. You'll do before-and-after drafts from your own inbox.

11

Minutes, reports and documents

Turning messy notes into clean minutes and writing a briefing note from scratch. You'll also outline a funding proposal or report you can keep building on.

12

Reading long documents

Long policies and fifty-page reports. You'll get a plain summary with every deadline pulled out, then ask it questions about what's inside.

13

Spreadsheets and numbers

Cleaning up a messy list and writing the formula you can never remember. You can use the sample files we bring or your own.

14

Meetings and planning

Agendas, prep notes, follow-up emails, and breaking a big project into steps with dates and owners.

Block 4 · Safe and sticky

End of the day. Staying safe with it and making it a habit.

15

Checking the output

How to catch the wrong date or the source that doesn't exist. You'll set up a checking routine that matches the stakes, quick for small jobs and careful for anything that goes public.

16

Privacy and workplace rules

What never goes into a public AI tool, and how the placeholder method works. We also cover working inside your organization's AI policy, or helping write one if there isn't one yet.

17

Ethics and when to leave AI out

Plagiarism, giving credit, when to tell people AI helped, and the jobs a person should do start to finish.

18

Your 18-day plan

Before you leave you set up your 18-day challenge and your own prompt library, so day 1 starts the next morning.

After the course

The 18-day challenge.

What you learn in a training day fades fast if you don't use it, so the course keeps going after the day is over. Starting the next morning, you get one skill a day for eighteen days. Each day pairs that skill with one tool and a real job from your week, and it takes about fifteen minutes.

How it works

Each day's skill and practice task is in the student portal at getwithitai.net. You write down what you tried and how it went, and the tracker shows your streak.

Your instructor checks in

Your instructor reads your notes during the challenge and helps when something isn't working. By day 18 you've got a routine and a prompt library built from your own work.

Questions

Questions people ask.

What should I bring?

A laptop or tablet with a charger, plus a few real examples of your work, like an email you've been dreading or messy meeting notes. Leave private details out. Module 16 shows you how to work with that kind of material safely.

I've never used an AI app. Is that a problem?

That's fine. Module 1 assumes you haven't. People starting fresh often do the best because they have no bad habits to unlearn.

Do I need to pay for an AI app?

The free versions cover everything in the course. Module 4 tells you when a paid plan is worth it for your kind of work.

How much time does the 18-day challenge take?

About fifteen minutes a day. It's built around work you were going to do anyway, so you get most of that time back.

Can my whole team take it together?

Yes. We run private sessions for up to 15 people with examples from your workplace. Email getwithitai@protonmail.com with your headcount and what your team does.
