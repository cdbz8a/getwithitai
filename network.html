Knowledge Keeper

How sharing works.

The computer in the band office holds the archive. Anyone who works there can sit down and use it. Other communities see what you send them. The public sees what you mark public.

Who can use it

Walk in. Sit at the desk. Look things up or add a record. You use this computer. Staff can lock it at night with one shared code if they want.

Where it lives

The records are on that office computer, and on the copies you made. Someone at home opening this website sees a blank archive on their own laptop.

When something leaves

A teaching stays in the office until someone marks it. Mark it for another community, or mark it public. Family and restricted records stay in the office.

Sharing

You share by sending a file.

If two communities want to share, they talk first. Then they mark the records that may travel, export a file, and send it the way they already send sensitive things. A USB is enough.

The other office imports the file. We do not keep a copy.

Two kinds of file

For another community: only records marked to go to trusted offices. Restricted and family records stay out.

For the public: only records someone marked public on purpose, for a poster, a handout, or a page on a website. Everything else stays put.

A full backup is different. That is a copy of everything on that computer, for safekeeping in another building.

If the building is gone

Keep copies in more than one place.

One computer is a working copy. Keep another copy in another building, on a drive in a drawer, or with a trusted office in another community.

Write down where those copies live, so the next person knows where to look. The files are ordinary text. You can open them later without our company.

If you already use Syncthing, it can keep two office folders in sync. If you already use IPFS, you can pin a shareable file you control. You run those tools. A USB between buildings is enough.
