Knowledge Keeper

Keep what people know.

Elders hold stories, language, land knowledge, and how the work was done. Some of that is on paper. A lot of it is only in someone’s head. Knowledge Keeper is a way for a community to write it down and keep it on a computer in the office.

Why this exists

Paper fades. People pass on.

Notebooks sit in a drawer. A recording sits on a phone. An auntie can still name every plant on the trail, and nobody is writing while she talks. When that person is gone, the knowledge goes with them.

The community decides who collects a teaching, who may hear it, and where the copy lives.

Who decides

The community sets the rules.

People in the community can use AI for ordinary work if they want to. Language, ceremony, and family knowledge stay in the Nation’s hands until the community says otherwise.

Ownership

The knowledge belongs to the people it came from. GetWithItAI does not own it. A model-maker does not own it.

Control

The community’s protocol decides who sits in the interview, what gets asked, whether a recording is made, and who edits the transcript.

Access

Who may hear or read a record is written on the record. Restricted means only the people named in the protocol.

Where the files live

Records live on a computer or drive the community controls. You export a file to a USB when you want a copy you can lock in a cabinet.

For First Nations work in Canada we follow the First Nations principles of OCAP®: Ownership, Control, Access, Possession. OCAP® is a registered trademark of the First Nations Information Governance Centre (FNIGC). The official definition is on their site: fnigc.ca/ocap-training. Reading this page is our own work.

OCAP® is a First Nations standard. Inuit, Métis, and other Aboriginal communities have their own laws and protocols. We follow yours.

Free basics tutorials

Copies

Keep the archive in more than one place.

The computer in the band office is where people work day to day. Keep another copy somewhere else, in a file you can open later without us.

Anyone in the office can use it.

The archive is on the band-office computer. Staff sit down and use it. They use one shared computer, so they do not each need an account.

New records stay in the office. If a teaching should go to another community, or onto a poster, someone marks that record and exports a file.

This office

Day-to-day work happens here. Clearing the browser can wipe it, so you export a copy.

Another building

A USB or drive in the health centre, the school, or a fireproof box in a different hall. Write it on the replica list so people remember it exists.

Another community

A trusted office can hold a full backup, or only the records you marked to share.

Copies in more than one place, in ordinary files, are what keep the archive available. We do not keep a master database. The files stay with you.

Sharing with other communities

You share by sending a file.

If two communities want to share, they pick which records may travel and send a file. Restricted and family records stay out of that file.

How the file gets there

Walk a USB over. Use the email you already trust for sensitive files. Or keep two office folders in sync with Syncthing. Import it on the other computer. The same record will not duplicate.

What stays in the office

Ceremony that stays in the room. Names that stay local. Anything marked restricted or family. A public AI tool only sees a record if you copy that record out by hand after the community has said it may go.

If you already use IPFS, you can pin a shareable file on a node you run.

A capture day

Talk first. Type second.

Before anyone hits record

  • Who has authority to say this teaching may be kept.
  • Whether it may be written, recorded, or only spoken in the room.
  • Who may hear it later: family, community, researchers, or people in the room only.
  • Whether a public AI tool may see it. The usual answer is no.
  • Where the original paper or tape will live after the day.

In the room

  • The knowledge holder talks. We go at their pace.
  • Paper gets photographed or typed, with a note of where the original is and what condition it is in.
  • They correct the first transcript. Their words win.
  • Restricted teachings are marked restricted on the record.

If they want to talk

Start here.

These are conversation starts. Skip any that do not fit. The holder leads.

On paper

What is this paper? Who wrote it? When? Where has it lived? What would be lost if this page disappeared? Is there anything on it that should stay uncopied?

In the head

If you had one afternoon with a young person from here, what would you make sure they knew how to do? What names, places, or plants should they be able to recognize? What do you wish someone had written down when you were young?

How we work

Community records stay with the community.

  • A public chat tool only sees a record if the community has decided that, in writing, for that record.
  • You hold the files. We leave with what you agreed we could take, which is usually nothing.
  • We are accountable to the Nation in front of us. OCAP® belongs to FNIGC.
  • We work from what people bring into the room, with their say-so.
Where do the records go when I type them here?

They stay in this browser on this computer. If you clear the browser, you can lose them. That is why you export a file to a community drive. We cannot see what you type here.

Can we use AI to help type a recording?

If the community says that record may leave the room, and only with a tool they approve. The archive’s usual setting is to keep the record away from public AI tools.
