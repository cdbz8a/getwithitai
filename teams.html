The problem

Most teams are using a fraction of what they pay for.

Everyone picked their own tool

Some people use ChatGPT, some use Copilot, some use nothing. Nobody agreed on a tool, and nobody said what's allowed in. Client lists, pricing and staff details end up typed into free personal accounts, and you'd never know.

The people using it barely scratch the surface

A one-line request, whatever comes back, and the verdict that "it's not very good." Meanwhile the features that save real time, like instructions, memory, files and templates, sit there unopened.

It isn't a software problem. Nobody has sat down with your people, on their own work, and shown them how. That's what we do.

Questions

Questions teams ask.

Our team isn't technical.

That's who this is for. If they can write an email, they can do this. Everything is in plain English and practiced on their own work.

We already have Copilot. Why would we need training?

Because having it and using it well are different things. Most teams use a small part of what's already installed. We open it up with them, on their own documents and inbox.

Half my team thinks AI is useless.

Usually because their first try was a one-line request with a bland answer. Once they see it handle a real task of theirs, properly briefed, that changes quickly.

We tried a session before and everyone forgot it by Friday.

That's why everyone leaves with their own saved templates, the material is sent afterwards, and full-day groups do the 18-day challenge with check-ins.

Can you help us write an AI policy?

Yes. Every format covers what stays out of AI, and our consulting work includes a short, plain-language set of rules for your team.

Can you train online?

Yes. In person works best for hands-on groups, and online works well for teams spread across sites.
