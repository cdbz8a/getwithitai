Trades

Built for tradespeople.

The work is the job. Paperwork eats the evening. We teach you to draft estimates, customer texts, and invoices in the way you already talk on site. You still put the dollar amounts in.

Talk to us Pricing

Estimates and quotes

Turn job notes into a customer-facing estimate: what’s included, what’s extra, two options. You still set the numbers. The model leaves prices blank unless you typed them.

Customer texts

“We’re 40 minutes out.” “Rain delay, next window Thursday if dry.” Short, like you actually talk on site.

Notes into invoice language

What you did, in language the customer can read. You check the line items before it goes out.

What stays in your books

Full names with addresses, account numbers, exact invoices, health notes. Use Role A, Client B, and ranges. Add the real details back in your own email.

Example. This is a draft, not a price quote.

Job notes into something you can send

What you typed at 9 p.m.

kitchen tap leaking, maybe cartridge, extra if seized, tomorrow afternoon?

Customer-facing draft

We’ll replace the kitchen faucet cartridge. If the shutoff is seized, that’s extra labour. We’ll stop and tell you before we add it. Two windows: tomorrow 1–3 or Thursday 8–10. Parts are extra. We’ll show the receipt.

You still put the dollar amounts in. Use the prompt generator with a trades starter, or paste this:

You are a trades writer. Turn these job notes into a customer text under 90 words. Plain language. Two schedule options. Leave prices blank unless I typed them. Flag anything I still have to fill in. Notes: [PASTE]

Questions tradespeople actually ask

Will this make me look like a call centre?

Add one sentence the way you talk on site. That’s the whole skill. You send the second draft, not the first.

Can I paste the customer’s address?

Use “the house on 4th” or Client A in a public tool. Addresses, account numbers, and exact invoices stay in your own books.
